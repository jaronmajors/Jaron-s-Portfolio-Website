Braided Body Fatigue Characterization

Abbott Laboratories — Cardiac Rhythm Management Product Development Engineering Co-op Sylmar, CA

Summary

The AVEIR CSP leadless pacemaker uses a composite braided body for stiffness around a fatigue- resistant conductor coil. The original acute, first-in-human design wasn't built for chronic fatigue conditions, and the polymer in the braided body was expected to behave differently once soaked in a clinically relevant fluid environment versus tested dry in air. I led testing to characterize how the braided body actually holds up under cyclic bending in both conditions, to build a defensible fatigue strategy ahead of a pivotal IDE submission.

What I Did

  • Designed test fixturing and defined sample preparation and conditioning steps from scratch, since no established method existed for this component.
  • Ran cyclic bend fatigue testing on a Bose ElectroForce 3000 machine — 10 samples in air and 6 samples soaked in water, at two curvatures chosen to simulate a high-stress clinical state.
  • Tracked failure mode and cycles-to-failure for each sample, capturing photos and observations throughout testing to catch outliers and support later analysis.
  • Compared failure modes between conditions: samples in air consistently broke through a linear Elasthane fracture, while soaked samples showed a distinct "lightning bolt" failure from wiring pushing out through the braid.
  • Iterated on fixturing, test conditions, and method setup multiple times to get consistent, reliable data.

Conclusions & Results

  • Samples tested in air fully failed at 154,000 cycles on average, with the Elasthane fracturing before the braid.
  • Samples tested soaked in water fully failed at 1.57 million cycles on average — roughly a 3–10x improvement in fatigue life at high stress compared to air testing.
  • This confirmed that clinically relevant (soaked) conditions are meaningfully less damaging than dry air testing, and that testing should be run in simulated clinical environments to avoid overly conservative results.
  • The test method and results became the foundation for the braided body's fatigue strategy heading into the pivotal IDE submission, with results expected to be even more favorable at the lower frequencies and stress states seen in actual use.
  • Set next steps: run flex fatigue at additional frequencies and stress states, and pair testing with FEA to quantify safety factors.
