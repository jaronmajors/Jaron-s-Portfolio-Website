Leadless Pacemaker Loading Tool

Abbott Laboratories — Cardiac Rhythm Management R&D Engineering Intern Sylmar, CA

Summary

Abbott's AVEIR CSP leadless pacemaker introduced a new ball-cone tethering mechanism between the catheter and the device, replacing a legacy tethering approach that had caused implant reliability issues. That new mechanism needed a purpose-built loading tool: something that would guide a physician through a consistent, repeatable connection between the catheter and pacemaker, protect the device from damage during handling, and hold up under real procedural conditions. I worked within the core loading tool team to take that tool from concept through a validated, production-ready prototype ahead of an IDE submission.

What I Did

  • Ran an agile, iterative design cycle — starting from 50+ early concepts, narrowing to a small set of finalists through core-team design sessions, Pugh matrix scoring, and in-house SME review.
  • Leveraged the legacy loading tool platform through 60+ iterations to minimize cost and lead time, focusing changes on the latch and docking cap interface.
  • Built 40+ rapid prototype versions to iterate on form and tolerances under live feedback.
  • Developed and ran mechanical characterization tests on an Instron — latch activation force and docking cap insertion force — and compared results against CAD-based FEA to drive design decisions.
  • Partnered with Human Factors to run and support 11+ physician usability studies for preliminary design validation.
  • Designed test methods, fixtures, and procedures to make sure prototype features would translate cleanly to production-grade, injection-molded parts, working directly with mold vendors on manufacturability.
  • Documented drawings, change orders, and technical reports in Windchill PLM to support the design history file.

Conclusions & Impact

  • Finalized a prototype with four key improvements: better latch feedback, a raised ball funnel for guidance, an outlined docking cap cradle, and a potential colorant indicator for orientation.
  • Strengthened relationships with key opinion leader physicians through hands-on usability sessions, and improved the overall physician experience with the device.
  • Achieved the design goal of minimal cost and change impact by building on the legacy tool platform rather than starting from a clean sheet.
  • Set up next steps for the program: mold vendor discussions, design characterization and verification testing on production-intent mold parts, and a bio-compatibility assessment ahead of the IDE submission deadline.
  • Biggest takeaway: cross-functional input (engineering, human factors, manufacturing, and quality) needs to be weighed together from the start, and under tight timelines, prioritization and trade-off analysis make or break the schedule.
