Lower Limb Exoskeleton Assist Project (LLEAP)

Cal Poly LLEAP Structures Technical Lead San Luis Obispo, CA January 2024 – Present

What is LLEAP?

LLEAP is a multi-year Cal Poly club project dedicated to designing and building an exoskeleton that helps paraplegic individuals walk, used together with hand crutches. The project started in 2019 and has carried three prototypes through to completion so far. Joining as a freshman has been one of the defining parts of my college experience — I'm now entering my third year with the club, and it's shaped how I think about taking a design from an idea to something a person can actually put on and use. The club is just as focused on being an educational, hands-on environment as it is on the exoskeleton itself, giving members a place to apply what they're learning in class to a real physical system with real constraints.

LLEAP splits into a mechanical side and a software side. On mechanical, the hip and knee teams generate high torque in the smallest, lightest package possible to replicate those joints; linkages and structures connect everything together and keep the user stable and comfortable; and the ankle/foot team builds a base flexible enough to mimic natural ankle movement while supporting the user's full weight. Software covers mechatronics, sensing/UI, and simulation. Every sub-team stays in sync through biweekly meetings and documentation, and the club runs multiple design reviews a year in front of club members, engineering faculty, and industry sponsors.

What I Did

  • Led the Structures sub-team through the full ideation-to-manufacturing cycle for the exoskeleton's structural frame — the part that holds and connects the electronics housing, hip motors, straps, and padding — applying DFM/DFA principles at every design decision.
  • Designed the load path in CAD around braided carbon fiber square tubing and CNC-machined 6061 aluminum joints, sized to cut weight and part count versus the previous prototype while keeping the structure serviceable for future iterations.
  • Ran hand calculations and FEA (SolidWorks Simulation / Ansys) on the tubing, joints, fasteners, epoxy bonds, and the hip attachment mounting plate to hit target safety factors before committing anything to the shop.
  • Fabricated and machined structural components directly — cutting and bonding carbon fiber tubing, and milling aluminum joints and the hip attachment plate on a 3-axis CNC.
  • Wrote the manufacturing plans and bills of materials for the structures and soft-goods subsystems, and tracked the design against size, weight, and strength targets set against the prior prototype.
  • Delegated and managed tasks across the sub-team, and coordinated with the hip, knee, ankle, and software sub-teams so the structure stayed compatible with everyone else's parts as designs moved.
  • Presented at LLEAP's design reviews in front of club leadership, engineering faculty, and industry sponsors.
  • Separately, as Facilities Tech Manager, managed the club's workspace and equipment — FDM printers and hand tools — and gave manufacturing and prototyping guidance to other members' sub-teams.

Skills & What I Learned

LLEAP is the project that pushed me furthest past just the design — it's where I learned what it actually takes to carry something from a CAD model to a part on a shelf that has to survive a real person's weight, and to keep a team of student engineers with wildly different subsystems moving in the same direction at once.
