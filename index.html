SHEET 01 About

Design, verify, and get it right the first time.

I'm a mechanical engineering student at Cal Poly, San Luis Obispo, graduating December 2026, with two terms of hands-on R&D experience at Abbott's Cardiac Rhythm Management division and a summer designing flying-car components at Alef Aeronautics. My work spans concept CAD, rapid prototyping, and — increasingly — the rigorous testing that proves a design actually holds up: fatigue cycling, tensile pull testing, Instron and Bose machine characterization, and design verification for Class III medical devices.

I like problems where creativity and precision both matter — where a clever concept still has to survive a force curve, a tolerance stack, or a physician usability study. Outside of coursework I lead structures for Cal Poly's exoskeleton team (LLEAP) and support Cal Poly Racing's aerodynamics group and shop facilities.

FIG. 02
Abbott CRM · Co-op

Braided Body Fatigue Characterization

Characterized a novel braided lead body under chronic fatigue conditions on a Bose ElectroForce machine, comparing cycles-to-failure and failure modes in air versus soaked environments across two stress states. Found a 3–10x improvement in soaked conditions, informing the fatigue strategy for a pivotal IDE submission.

  • Bose ElectroForce
  • Fatigue Testing
  • Failure Analysis
  • Test Method Dev.

FIG. 03
Abbott CRM · Co-op

Braided Body Tensile Pull Testing

Designed and ran Instron tensile pull testing on braided lead bodies with 25% and 50% fracture depths against undamaged controls, to confirm retrieval strength holds up in a worst-case fracture scenario. Measured a 19–57% strength reduction depending on damage depth, setting a design input for future submissions.

  • Instron Tensile Testing
  • Fixture Design
  • Statistical Analysis
  • Design Inputs
